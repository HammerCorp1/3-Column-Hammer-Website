Hammer vs Fullpath: which AI should a dealership use?

By the Hammer team · Reviewed November 9, 2026

Fullpath is a customer data platform for dealerships. It markets to the customers you already have. For answering new leads, Hammer is the better tool: a text in under 5 seconds on Marketplace, Messenger, ads, CarGurus, OEM, and the rest, booked into the CRM you already have, starting at $99/mo.

Database marketing and lead response are two different jobs. Fullpath finds the next buyer in your data. Hammer answers the buyer who just asked.

This page is written by the Hammer team. Fullpath facts are from Fullpath's own site and Cox Automotive's press releases.

The short version

Use Hammer when new leads sit. Every connected lead gets a text in under 5 seconds. Follow-up keeps going until they book. The appointment lands in VinSolutions, DealerSocket, eLead, CDK, Reynolds, DriveCentric, Tekion, or DealerCenter. Live in under 48 hours.

Fullpath (formerly AutoLeadStar) pulls a dealer's CRM, DMS, and website data into one customer data platform, then runs email, SMS, and ad campaigns from it: equity campaigns, VIN-specific ads, and audience targeting. Cox Automotive completed its purchase of Fullpath on June 1, 2026, so it now sits in the same company as VinSolutions, Dealer.com, and Autotrader. Fullpath does not publish a price.

If the goal is more appointments out of an old database, a CDP is the tool. If the goal is never letting a new lead sit, that is Hammer.

Why a data platform does not fix speed to lead

A campaign brings a customer back. Then that customer sends a message, and somebody still has to answer it in seconds.

Hammer is that answer. After hours included. Playbooks you change yourself. A human can pause the AI or let it resume.

Stores that run a CDP still need a first-reply layer. Hammer is that layer.

What Hammer is not

Not a customer data platform. Not a replacement CRM. Not an AI voice caller. Your team still closes.
