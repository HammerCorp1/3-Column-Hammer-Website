Hammer vs AutoEngage: which AI should a dealership use?

By the Hammer team · Reviewed November 9, 2026

AutoEngage is AI service retention. It texts service customers from your DMS history and books the visit. For sales lead response, Hammer is the better tool: a text in under 5 seconds on Marketplace, Messenger, ads, CarGurus, OEM, and the rest, booked into the CRM you already have, starting at $99/mo.

AutoEngage works the service drive. Hammer works the sales lead. They are not the same purchase.

This page is written by the Hammer team. AutoEngage facts are from AutoEngage's own pricing and integrations pages.

The short version

Use Hammer for sales leads. Every connected lead gets a text in under 5 seconds. Follow-up keeps going until they book. The appointment lands in VinSolutions, DealerSocket, eLead, CDK, Reynolds, DriveCentric, Tekion, or DealerCenter. Live in under 48 hours.

AutoEngage's AI, Lisa, finds likely service customers in DMS history, handles the text conversation, and books the visit directly into CDK, Reynolds, Tekion, Dealertrack, PBS, DealerBuilt, or Xtime. It publishes a price: from $1,895 per rooftop per month, plus setup fees and contract terms.

If your gap is service customers who stopped coming back, AutoEngage is built for that. If your gap is sales leads that sit, that is Hammer, at a fraction of the price.

Why the department matters

Service retention tools start from the DMS and reach out. Lead response starts from the inbox and answers. A store can need one, the other, or both.

Most independent lots do not run a franchise DMS or a service drive big enough for a $1,895 per rooftop program. Their leaks are Marketplace, CarGurus, and Facebook ad leads after close.

Hammer is built for that inbox. After hours included. Playbooks you change yourself. A human can pause the AI or let it resume.

What Hammer is not

Not a service retention program. Not a replacement CRM. Not an AI voice caller. Your team still closes.
